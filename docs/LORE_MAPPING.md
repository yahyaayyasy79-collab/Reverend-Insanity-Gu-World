### Hunger

A literal per-Gu hunger meter (a number that drains every turn) cannot be stored in Unciv's ruleset: units have no
counters. The engine does offer **timed statuses** and a trigger when a status runs out, which gives the same
behaviour [src `UnitTurnManager.startTurn`, `MapUnit.removeStatus`]:

1. **Fed:** the Gu works normally.
2. **Weakened:** as soon as its food is in deficit, the Gu's effects switch off (each effect carries
   `<when above [amount] [stat/resource]>` on its food), and the engine adds its own −25% for a missing resource.
3. **Hungry:** at the first turn start in deficit the unit gets a hunger status for that food (Orchid, Pork, Jade or
   Wine Hunger): 3 turns for a rank-1 Gu, 5 for a rank-2 Gu.
4. **Dying:** if the food is still short when the status runs out (after 2 more turns for rank 1, 4 for rank 2), the
   Gu dies with a 50% chance; a survivor gets the status again at the next turn start and rolls again 3 (rank 1) or 5
   (rank 2) turns later. Under a lasting deficit a rank-1 Gu lives 5 turns on average, a rank-2 Gu 9. Rank-1 Gu eat
   often in canon (a Moonlight Gu twice a day), rank-2 Gu rarely (White Jade Gu every 20 days), so the higher rank
   holds out longer.
5. **Recovered:** once the food is back, the status removes itself and the Gu works again.

**Why a roll and not a fixed death (round 4, your option B).** Gaining or losing a Gu does not refresh the civ's
resource count [src], so a fixed death would kill every holder of that food at the same turn start, even when one
death would close the gap. The roll is seeded per unit (its name, tile and movement) and per turn [src
`Conditionals.getStateBasedRandom`, `GameContext.hashCode`], so holders die one by one and the deficit can close
before all of them are gone. **Tuning rule (yours):** the autoplay's rank-1 hunger death rate decides whether the
rank-1 threshold rises from 2 turns to 3 or 4; no manual tuning before that data exists.

A killer move whose rank-2 Gu died goes at the next turn start, and the unit can climb the ladder again.

## Path choice: why two paths, and which

**Spec tension.** §6 S5 suggests about six paths for Slice 1; §8 allows two path branches. §8 is the hard budget, so
Slice 1 ships two and the rest become the roadmap.

**Chosen: Moon Path (月道, your design choice) and Strength Path (力道, canon), mutually exclusive.**

1. The anchors. Moonlight Gu fed on moon-orchid petals is the Gu Yue emblem (canon); the novel gives it no path, so
   the moon path is an adaptation you chose. The strength path is canon (the Wu clan's leader walks it), and the boar
   Gu are early beast-strength Gu on it (one branch, not its archetype). The Xiong emblem is the Bear Strength Gu.
2. Two combat styles (round 5c): Strength hits hardest in melee; Moon is a lighter melee skirmisher that throws a moon
   blade before every clash with a unit (40% of its strength, `Before engaging in combat performs an extra ranged attack
   with [amount]% of melee combat strength`). Both can take cities; a ranged Moon could not, so Moon clans never won a
   war in autoplay (docs/AUTOPLAY.md, round 5c).
3. Opposite pressure on the economy: Moon costs fewer stones (+75/150/225% upkeep) and eats Moon Orchid; Strength
   costs more (+100/175/275%) and eats Wild Boar. Both need Jade for their rank-2 fusion.
4. Dao marks: +5% strength per adopted own-branch policy; a cross-path Gu on a unit gives −10%; only path units reach
   ranks 2–4, so an unpathed clan stalls at Rank One with a Jade Skin Gu.
5. AI split two against two: Gu Yue and Bai lean Moon (they compete for orchids); Xiong and Wu lean Strength (they
   compete for pork). Everyone competes for jade.

**Later candidates:** Enslavement, Blood, Water/Ice (Bai Ning Bing), Earth, Lightning, Wind, Wood, Light (Little Light
Gu), Wisdom, Time (Spring Autumn Cicada), Theft, Poison, Fire.

## Pillar coverage (human and AI)

**P1 Power is expensive to keep.** Every unit of a major civ pays upkeep (`[-3] units cost no maintenance <for [Major]
Civilizations>` cancels the engine's three free units). Rank multipliers: Rank One +25%; Moon +75/+150/+225%; Strength
+100/+175/+275%. Every Gu eats its canon food through `Consumes` on the promotion: Moonlight Gu → Moon Orchid; boar Gu →
Wild Boar (pork); Jade Skin Gu → Jade; White Jade Gu → 2 Jade (the fusion swaps pork for more jade, as in canon);
Moon Raiment → Moon Orchid; Liquor Worm → Wine. An unfed Gu goes through the [hunger meter](#hunger): weakened at
once, then a 50% death roll after 2 (rank 1) or 4 (rank 2) more turns of deficit, repeated every 3 or 5 turns. Stones come from rare Spirit Springs, so a clan's
upkeep capacity depends on the springs it holds. Further tiers: stones below 0 (−20% strength and Stone Shortage at
25% per turn), the engine's disband at −200. *AI:* the same formula times `aiUnitMaintenanceModifier`; every Gu grant
is gated on a food surplus, but the AI promotes all its units on one stale resource count [src], so it does overdraw;
the hunger meter then clears the deficit instead of leaving it forever. Ration (weight 2) is its way out of a stone
shortage.

**P2 Every breakthrough is a gamble.** Aptitude Awakening rolls a weighted die on every new Gu Master (A/B/C/D
5/20/35/40%). Refine Gu is the only promotion bought with XP, so all XP becomes gambles: careful, reckless, a Liquor
Worm lure, or cooling off. Fusions are harder than single rungs (70%/40% instead of 85%/50%); a spare wild boar is the
canon tusk catalyst (+20 points for White Jade Gu). The Flower Wine Monk Inheritance is a double-edged prize.
*AI:* buys Refine Gu like any promotion and resolves the Alert by weights; Refine Gu and the refining choices close in
every state where no rung can be gained (14,742 states checked), so the AI never pays for an empty attempt.

**P3 Heaven and earth are without benevolence.** Beast Tides hit every major at random (3% per turn after T30, +2%
after T90; random timing staggers civs, E6) and escalate by era up to each civ's own Thunder Crown Wolf. Lairs spawn
Lightning Wolves, and Bold Lightning Wolves once every civ knows Rank Three Cultivation [src]. Inheritances can trap.
Preparation (Formation Platform, a stone reserve) opens better choices. *AI:* everything fires automatically.

**P4 Specialists beat dabblers.** Moon and Strength exclude each other; path units require their branch; unpathed
clans cap at Rank One + Jade Skin Gu; cross-path Gu cost −10%; dao marks reward depth. *AI:* personalities put one path
first (Gu Yue Moon 10, Bai Moon 8, Xiong Strength 10 and starts with it adopted, Wu Strength 10).

**P5 Righteous and demonic are strategies.** The branches exclude each other. Righteous: −25% upkeep on friendly land,
city-state favour, defence, clan law. Demonic: stones from every kill, plunder, the Lone Cultivators policy (a small
cheap elite core, the S6 rogue strategy), the strike-first and caravan-robbery choices; paid for with faster
city-state decay, unrest and a 3% Betrayal chance per turn. *AI:* Wu starts Righteous; Xiong leans
Demonic; personalities set the rest.

## Refinement ladder

**Cultivation (round 5d).** Every Gu Master standing in a city with a Gu Academy (or Wu's Martial Arena, which
replaces it) gains 2 XP per turn until it holds a Killer Move: the academy's seclusion pays for the next attempt.
Before, XP came only from battle, so the AI bought one attempt per unit and the ladder stalled at rung 1 (autoplay,
King, 200 turns: 0.7 rank-2 Gu and 0.04 Killer Moves per clan; with cultivation 5.1 and 2.8, and the stones and the
hunger meter both bite harder; docs/AUTOPLAY.md, round 5d).

Refine Gu opens the Refinement Attempt Alert on the unit. The careful and reckless choices carry the same ladder with
different odds. Each unit state matches **at most one** "gains" line, so one attempt rolls once; lines fire top to
bottom and promotion conditionals are read live [src], so one attempt climbs at most one rung. After a successful
fusion, deterministic "loses" lines remove the two ingredients.

| Unit | Rung 1 (85% / 50%) | Rung 2 — canon fusion (70% / 40%) | Rung 3 (85% / 50%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moon Gu Master | Moonlight Gu (needs a Moon Orchid surplus) | Moon Raiment = Moonlight Gu + Jade Skin Gu (a spare Jade as material unless a Jade Skin is carried); a defensive Gu that helps nearby allies | Killer Move (Killer Moves tech) |
| Strength Gu Master | White Boar Gu (needs a Wild Boar surplus) | White Jade Gu = White Boar Gu + Jade Skin Gu (1 spare Jade with a carried Jade Skin, 2 without); a spare Wild Boar is the tusk: 90% / 60% | Killer Move (Killer Moves tech) |
| Rank One Gu Master (any clan) | Jade Skin Gu (needs a Jade surplus): the cap | — | — |

**Off the ladder** (never named by a ladder line, so they add no state): Black Boar Gu (Jia ally, stacks as a second
boar), the clan emblems Bear Strength Gu and Stream Gu, Gu Yue's emblem Moonlight Gu on a Strength unit (only −10%),
and the Liquor Worm (Rank One only; it leaves at the next turn start after a breakthrough).

**Dead states.** The refining choices close when the unit has Killer Move; holds a rank-2 Gu before the Killer Moves
tech; or lacks the food its next rung needs (orchid, pork, or 1–2 jade). An unpathed Rank One is closed once it holds
Jade Skin Gu or when there is no Jade. Refine Gu itself closes in all of these states, except that a Rank One can
still buy an attempt when it can lure a Liquor Worm. It also closes below 50 HP; the reckless choice closes below
31 HP. The Unfinished Refinement ruin carries the same guard, so it is never drawn for a unit that cannot gain
anything. **Hunger:** a Gu whose food is in deficit is weakened and, after 2 (rank 1) or 4 (rank 2) more turns of
deficit, dies with a 50% chance per roll; a Killer Move whose rank-2 Gu died goes at the next turn start, and the unit can climb again.

## Balance starting numbers

Starting points for Phase 2; every tunable number goes into `docs/BALANCE.md` once JSON exists, and the 100-turn
autoplay (MOD_SPEC §10, §11) decides.

**Unit upkeep in stones per turn**, from the engine formula [src `getUnitMaintenance`]: cost = (0.5 × Σ unit
weights × (1+p))^(1+p/3), p = turn / 500 on standard speed; weights multiply. Majors pay for every unit.

| Situation | Round 1 | Round 2 |
|---|---|---|
| T40, careful Moon player: 2 laborers, 3 Rank One, 1 Rank Two Moon with its rung-1 Gu | 4 | 4 |
| T40, careless: 2 laborers, 5 Rank Two Strength | 6 | 6 |
| T80, Xiong AI: 2 laborers, 2 Rank Three + 3 Rank Two Strength with their rung-2 Gu, 2 Rank One with an emblem Gu, 2 recruited wolves | 14 | 12 |
| T120, 2 laborers + 6 elite Rank Four Strength (rung-2 Gu + Killer Move) | 28 | 23 |
| T120, 2 laborers + 6 elite Rank Four Moon (rung-2 Gu + Killer Move) | 24 | 20 |

Round 2 lowers stone upkeep for rank-2 units because their Gu now eat jade instead of stones (the old White Jade Gu
cost +20% upkeep). The early P1 pressure therefore rests on the Gu foods and on building and road upkeep; whether that
is enough is the autoplay's question. **Event costs never push a non-negative treasury below zero:** every
stone-costing choice is gated by a `<when above [amount] [stat/resource]>` at or above its cost.

**Known limits, accepted for Slice 1 and watched in autoplay:**

1. Gaining or fusing a Gu does not refresh the civ's resource count [src], and the AI promotes all its units in one
   pass, so overdraws are normal AI play (also after a pillaged camp or mine, or a lapsed trade). The holders are
   weakened until new food closes the gap or the hunger meter's rolls kill enough Gu to close it.
2. Jade is the rank-2 bottleneck for both paths. Sources: hill deposits, the Refinement Chamber (+1 per city) and the
   Flower Wine Monk Inheritance (+2). The AI never trades for promotion food, so a jade-poor AI plateaus at rung 1
   (without wasting XP).
3. One luxury (Furs), and the map gives a regional luxury to one major only [src]: happiness is tight. Every
   difficulty gets +6 base happiness as the starting fix; the autoplay decides.
4. The AI cannot see that the Flower Wine Monk Inheritance destroys its own Awakening Grounds; Gu Yue's AI is told
   never to build it (it would also lose the flower sea's +2 orchids), the other AIs may still build it.
5. Population loss stops at 1 [src]: "Abandon the outer fields" is free for size-1 colonies. Single-city costs use the
   capital (`[-1] population [in capital]`) because a civ-level "random city" always picks the same city [src].
6. With barbarians disabled, "Meet the wolves" and "Hunt the traitor" summon nothing, so they cost nothing.
7. Ration can stack if Stone Shortage fires again during its 8 turns; the three Betrayal triggers can fire in the same
   turn; Culture has no sink once both allowed branches are complete; reloading a save rerolls chance rolls (E5); the
   victory screen shows "Max Turns" only for a victory named "Time" [src].
8. Spirit Springs are rare by design. Without the Strategic Balance option a clan may find no spring near its first
   cities; it then has no Awakening Grounds (and fewer stones) until it settles or takes one. That is the intended
   geopolitics, but the autoplay must show the AI coping with it.

## How this plan was made

* **Round 1 (2026-10-12):** three independent drafts (lore, systems, player experience), a synthesis, and two skeptic
  reviews (lore, mechanics); their must-fixes and most should-fixes were applied (dead refinement states, double
  rolls, food gates, upkeep, population costs, CANON/ADAPTED tags). The Phase 0 claim E4 was corrected.
* **Round 2 (2026-10-13):** your answers were checked claim by claim against the raw novel text, each by a researcher
  and an independent skeptic. Two independent designs (lore fidelity, systems and AI) were then merged; the ladder was
  verified exhaustively and the whole plan by Unciv's own validator. A final lore skeptic and mechanics skeptic
  reviewed the result; their fixes are applied (starving Gu, the ruin guard, canon Moon Raiment, the Xiong survival,
  stone springs, happiness, AI weights).
* **Round 3 (2026-10-13):** your decisions: the Wu clan, the hunger meter (built on timed statuses after reading the
  engine source), Spirit Springs. Validated again with Unciv's own validator and the ladder check.
* **Round 4 (2026-10-13):** your option B for hunger deaths (a 50% roll per Gu, seeded per unit) and your approval:
  autoplay with Strategic Balance on decides the tuning.
